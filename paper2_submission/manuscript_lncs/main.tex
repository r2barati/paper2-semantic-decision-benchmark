\begin{abstract}
Retrieval is evaluated by relevance, and increasingly by the quality of the
text a language model then generates. A growing class of deployments instead
feeds retrieved evidence to a decision policy, where an error's cost depends on
which action it induces and on how the system evolves afterwards. The
observation that topical relevance can diverge from task value is textbook
information retrieval~\cite{borlund2003concept}, and recent retrieval-augmented
generation (RAG) evaluation has measured versions of it: relevance labels
correlate weakly with downstream performance~\cite{salemi2024erag}, grounding
utility is model-specific~\cite{grogu2026}, and distracting passages can hurt
more than random ones~\cite{cuconasu2024power,yoran2024making} --- with
irrelevant context distracting even strong models~\cite{shi2023large} while
retrieval helps mostly tail queries~\cite{mallen2023trust}. Selective
retrieval~\cite{jiang2023active,asai2023selfrag} and position
sensitivity~\cite{liu2023lost} are established remedies our design does not
implement. What those
studies do not provide is a frozen, regenerable instrument that scores evidence
by \emph{sequential realised return} under a fixed controller, with the loss
attributed across retrieval, interpretation and control. We build one: an
operational report feed, BM25 / dense / hybrid / reranked retrieval, naive and
provenance-aware LLM consumers plus a model-free rule-based reference, a
development-tuned no-text comparator (labeled extension, outside the frozen
matrix), a fixed receding-horizon controller, and a simulator that returns
realised operating profit over 160 held-out queries $\times$ 5 paired
seeds. The measured pattern is a mismatch, not a scarcity: for LLM consumers, real retrieval
reduces return relative to no-text operating points (all C1 arms $-41$ to
$-104$ and three of eight C3 cells, Holm-robust; remaining real-retriever
comparisons directionally negative but Holm-n.s., except random/C3/14B
$+11.0$, an abstention-fallback cell), while oracle-selected
evidence from
the same corpus adds up to $+178$; a relevance-to-factual refinement gap of $0.00$ and a
downstream control gap of $+456.8$ locate the loss. Relevance-only evaluation
cannot see the consumer dependence we measure: a retriever$\times$consumer
interaction that survives Holm correction for rule-vs-LLM contrasts. Every
table regenerates from stored episodes; all analysis code, model pins and
randomisation are frozen.
\keywords{Retrieval evaluation \and Downstream utility \and Evidence selection
\and Consumer dependence \and Negative results.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An operator monitoring a supply network receives a stream of reports. Most
concern other sites; some are stale; a few describe conditions that should
change what they order this period. Choosing which reports to act on is an
information access problem, and its consequences are sequential: an order
placed now arrives several periods later, into a state that earlier decisions
produced.

Standard retrieval evaluation scores such a system by topical relevance. That
topical relevance need not equal task value has been known for decades, from
situational relevance to task-based evaluation~\cite{borlund2003concept}, and
the RAG literature has recently quantified single-step versions of the
divergence: eRAG shows upstream relevance is a weak proxy for end-task
utility~\cite{salemi2024erag}; GroGU shows grounding utility differs per
consuming model~\cite{grogu2026}; strong retrievers can retrieve harder
distractors than weak ones~\cite{cuconasu2024power,yoran2024making}. None of
these gives IR a reusable way to score evidence by what it does to a
\emph{sequential} decision process with persisting state and asymmetric costs.
That instrument is this paper's contribution; the empirical pattern it reveals
is consistent with, and narrower than, the effects above.

We ask when retrieved text improves sequential decisions, and make the whole
path executable so the question can be answered rather than argued:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:path}
q,\,\mathcal{D} \;\longrightarrow\; R_k(q,\mathcal{D}) \;\longrightarrow\;
b(z\mid \cdot) \;\longrightarrow\; \pi(a_t\mid s_t,b) \;\longrightarrow\;
s_{t+1} \;\longrightarrow\; U.
\end{equation}

A retrieval system $R_k$ selects $k$ documents from a candidate pool
$\mathcal{D}$ under a fixed query $q$; a fixed consumer maps the selected
text to a belief $b$ over operational regimes; a fixed controller maps state and
belief to an action; a simulator returns realised utility $U$. Holding
everything after retrieval constant --- with shared seeds making each
query-seed cell a paired counterfactual across sources (demand draws and
regime paths are action-independent given the seed, so exogenous randomness
is exactly paired; histories diverge thereafter by design, and that
divergence IS the treatment effect) --- makes differences
in $U$ attributable to
evidence selection. Varying only the consumer, with retrieval held constant,
isolates consumption. An oracle ladder (Section~\ref{sec:ladder}) separates
selection from veracity from interpretation from control.

\paragraph{The result.}
At $k=3$, real retrieval reduces operating return relative to a no-retrieval
control for LLM consumers (BM25 $-103.5$ $[-137,-71]$, dense $-91.5$,
hybrid $-74.6$, rerank $-43.6$; all Holm-robust over the 105-arm family),
while oracle-selected evidence from the same corpus adds up to $+178.9$
$[+135,+221]$. A no-text belief tuned only on development queries reaches
$1881.1$ ($+102.1$ over the prior, $p<0.0002$): even the best constant policy
that reads nothing beats every real-retriever arm, so the harm finding survives a
tuned comparator. The loss decomposes cleanly: a relevance-to-factual
refinement gap of $0.00$ (both oracle sets saturate their consumer), an
interpretation gap that shrinks $140.5 \to 36.4$ from naive to
provenance-aware consumption, and a downstream control gap of $+456.8$ that
dominates everything even under perfect semantics.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A frozen, regenerable retrieval$\to$belief$\to$control benchmark scoring
evidence by sequential realised return, with retrieval quality on the same
episodes (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}).
(ii) A measured mismatch pattern --- real retrieval harms, oracle evidence
helps --- with a tuned no-text comparator, a rule-vs-LLM interaction, and a
four-gap attribution (Sections~\ref{sec:utility}--\ref{sec:ladder}).
(iii) A statistical discipline for this design: paired shared-seed worlds,
crossed bootstrap, Holm over 105 arms, dual priors, text-cluster honesty
check (Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}).
(iv) A reproducible artifact regenerating every table from stored episodes.

\paragraph{Scope.} Synthetic feed and environments; constructed, not
human-assessed, relevance (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). No new retrieval
model; no consumer or controller claimed preferable in general. The claim is
about \emph{evaluation} --- score decision-bound evidence at induced actions
and realised return, against a no-retrieval control and matched budget ---
scoped throughout to arms, priors and environments where corrections hold.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{What we inherit.}
That relevance can diverge from downstream value is established: situational
and task-based relevance critique~\cite{borlund2003concept}; eRAG's weak
relevance-to-utility correlation~\cite{salemi2024erag}; model-specific
grounding utility~\cite{grogu2026}; distracting-passage harm that grows with
retriever strength~\cite{cuconasu2024power,yoran2024making}; component-wise
RAG evaluation with uncertainty~\cite{saadfalcon2023ares,es2023ragas}. That
prediction quality differs from decision quality is established:
task-based end-to-end learning~\cite{donti2017task,elmachtoub2022smart,mandi2022rank}, value-aware dynamics~\cite{farahmand2017value,lambert2020objective}, decision calibration~\cite{zhao2021decision}, and the
classical value of information~\cite{howard1966information}. Probability
quality is standardly assessed with proper scores and calibration
diagnostics~\cite{guo2017calibration,degroot1983comparison,niculescu2005predicting}. \emph{None of these claims is ours:} each could have
explained our results, and the controls below test those explanations rather
than evading them. Closest are inventory decision-focused learning --- where
prior work trains \emph{through} a stochastic program, we freeze an
equivalent loop and vary only the supplied text --- and the SPO
loss~\cite{elmachtoub2022smart}, which already scores predictions by induced
decision error and which our $\Delta J$ applies to retrieved evidence; the
closest operational-language system instead keeps its LLM around the solver
as translator, not sensor~\cite{li2023optiguide}.

\paragraph{What remains novel here.}
We are not aware of prior work combining a frozen sequential controller and
world, retrieved natural-language evidence, matched budgets, paired
world$\times$language randomisation, and a six-node oracle ladder separating
selection, veracity, interpretation and control. In particular, no prior study
we know measures a rule-vs-LLM interaction on realised operating return, or a
tuned no-text policy beating every tested real retriever while oracle evidence
helps. The normalised value diagnostic is standard skill-score bookkeeping;
we claim no new metric.

\paragraph{Environment and baselines.}
The controlled simulator is a single-item lost-sales inventory model; the
secondary transfer uses a pinned published multi-echelon
package~\cite{gyminvmgmt}. Retrieval baselines are Okapi
BM25~\cite{robertson1994okapi} and cosine similarity over frozen
sentence embeddings~\cite{reimers2019sentencebert}, scored with
nDCG~\cite{jarvelin2002cumulated}.

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Objects and estimand}

Let $z$ be a latent operational regime, $\mathcal{D}$ a candidate pool of
reports and $q$ a fixed query expressing a standing information need. A
retrieval system returns an ordered list; its top $k$ documents are the
evidence. A fixed consumer maps that evidence to a belief $b(z)$, and a fixed
controller maps state and belief to an action.

Write $G_i(m)$ for the \emph{realised} return of episode $i$ under information
source $m$, $G_i(m)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} r(s^{(i)}_t,a^{(i)}_t,z^{(i)})$,
undiscounted over the fixed $T=40$ horizon. Its expectation is estimated by
the weighted benchmark return
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jw}
J_w(m) \;=\; \sum_{r} w_r \,
\operatorname*{mean}_{f \in \mathcal{F}_r} \;
\operatorname*{mean}_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{r,f}} \;
\operatorname*{mean}_{i \in \mathcal{S}} \; G_i(m; r, f, v),
\end{equation}
averaging seeds within a query $v$, queries within a family $f$, families
within a regime $r$, and combining regimes with declared weights $w_r$. The
\emph{balanced} estimand uses uniform $w_r$ and is primary; an
\emph{empirical} estimand matching the test bank mix (normal $0.2$, delay
$0.4$, surge $0.4$) is reported alongside every headline so that no
conclusion rests on the weighting choice. Group sizes are unequal, so
\eqref{eq:jw} is not an equal-row mean over episodes, and the difference is
numerically material.

Effects are reported as paired differences $\Delta J$ against a declared
reference: for retrieval, a control that retrieves nothing; for consumers,
the uninformed prior. Headline utility estimands use the 160 held-out test
queries; development queries enter episodes only for matrix coverage. Gaps
are signed differences of balanced $J$: retrieval $J(\text{actual}) -
J(\text{NoInfo})$, relevance-to-factual $J(\text{OracleFactual}) -
J(\text{OracleRelevant})$, interpretation $J(\text{PerfectBelief}) -
J(\text{OracleFactual})$, control $J(\text{Hindsight}) -
J(\text{PerfectBelief})$, with rungs defined in
Section~\ref{sec:ladder}. A development-tuned constant no-text belief
(Section~\ref{sec:tuned}) provides the stronger comparator. The normalised
diagnostic $\mathrm{SIVR} = (J(\text{condition}) - J(\text{NoInfo})) /
(J(\text{oracle-semantic}) - J(\text{NoInfo}))$ is reported against both
PerfectBelief and matched OracleFactual references with an explicit guard
(NaN and a marked status, never absolute normalisation, when the reference
gap is zero or negative); it is bookkeeping, not a new metric. With a fixed
heuristic controller, the value of information need not be non-negative: our
estimand is the value of information \emph{to this controller class}, not
universal value of information, so negative realised value diagnoses the
pairing, never a violation of decision theory. The \emph{perfect-semantic
reference} --- the true regime passed through the fixed controller --- is
demonstrably not an upper bound on realised return, so normalising by it
does not yield a recovered fraction of anything; we report raw paired
effects first and mark rather than interpret degenerate ratios.

\subsection{Uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

Queries are the primary unit; five paired seeds ($60000$--$60004$, disjoint
from all development ranges) quantify environmental uncertainty, shared
across every treatment. The design crosses worlds (seeds) with language
(queries): every query is evaluated on the same seeds, so we resample one
\emph{shared} seed sample per regime plus queries with replacement
(crossed bootstrap, $B=5{,}000$; $p$-values are centred test inversions
bounded below by $1/(B+1)$, never zero). Paired randomisation testing
follows IR significance-testing practice~\cite{smucker2007comparison}, and
family-wise control follows classifier-comparison
practice~\cite{demsar2006statistical}. Semantic statistics use paired
query bootstrap ($B=10{,}000$). Multiplicity is controlled by Holm
adjustment~\cite{holm1979simple}: over the 105-arm utility family (5 retrieval $\times$ 10 $+$
2 oracle $\times$ 10 $+$ 7 interventions $\times$ 5 --- every
(system, consumer, model, $k$) combination of the frozen matrix), and over ten preregistered
headline semantic cells; all other contrasts are estimation with CIs,
labeled exploratory. Three honesty provisions apply: (i) 200 IDs share 64
texts (63 test) --- a text-cluster bootstrap (median $1.08\times$, max $1.19\times$ wider; cluster-bootstrap practice
follows~\cite{cameron2008bootstrap})
accompanies every starred claim, which holds under both; effective
$N\approx137$; (ii) inference is to new queries/seeds \emph{within this
bank}, not new language; (iii) regime-conditional statements are exploratory
($n=5$ seeds).

\section{Retrieval to semantic transfer}
\label{sec:transfer}

Setup: 200 queries over a 64-node report feed (40 dev / 160 test;
regime-faithful pool judgments; test seal held until frozen analysis).
Systems: random, BM25, instructed dense, RRF hybrid ($k=120$), reranker over
hybrid top-30 (depth dev-selected), oracle-relevant/factual ceilings, seven
prespecified interventions. Consumers C0/C1/C3 (no C2 was ever defined): C0
(rule-based, model-free), C1 (naive
LLM use), C3 (per-document extraction, frozen abstention $\tau=0.5$, an arbitrary
predeclared constant with no ablation; C1 prompts for regime probabilities
plus event parameters as strict JSON, C3 for per-document entity, event,
freshness, stance and confidence; a frozen-payload sweep over
$\tau\in\{0.3,0.7\}$ leaves C3 accuracy and abstention unchanged to four
decimals, since per-document support avoids the threshold band (identical
beliefs imply identical downstream returns, so no utility rerun is needed);
abstentions carry the prior, score $0$ accuracy, keep prior Brier --- a
reject option in the classical sense~\cite{chow1970optimum}, without a
coverage-risk analysis~\cite{geifman2019selectivenet}, which we leave to
future work). Two pinned open-weight
models (8B/14B); 11{,}018 content-addressed cache files, zero assembly writes.

The ladder (Table~\ref{tab:gaps}, Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}) attributes the
loss rung by rung with controller, world, seeds and budget fixed:
\textsc{NoInfo} (prior), \textsc{ActualRetrieval} (frozen belief row),
\textsc{OracleRelevant} (top-$k$ by grade), \textsc{OracleFactual} (+fresh,
entity-matching, non-refuting), \textsc{PerfectBelief} (true-regime one-hot,
retrieval-independent), \textsc{HindsightOracle} (clairvoyant MILP) ---
separating selection, veracity, interpretation and control: the relevance-to-factual refinement gap is $0.00$ in every arm (both
oracle sets saturate their consumer identically); the interpretation gap
shrinks $140.5 \to 36.4$ from naive to provenance-aware consumption; the
downstream control gap ($+456.8$, bundling demand-foresight value with
controller suboptimality) dominates everything, so even perfect
semantics leave more than twice the perfect-semantics value on the table.
Regime slices (exploratory, per-regime paired CIs, no multiplicity control)
show the mechanism is concentrated, not diffuse:
reranked evidence helps substantially in delay worlds ($+150$--$+206$ across
consumers and models) while hurting in normal ($-225$--$-157$) and surge
($-129$--$-79$) worlds.

\paragraph{Interventions on oracle evidence.}
Perturbing the oracle-factual base seven prespecified ways (drop-decisive,
stale-swap, inject-contra, wrong-entity-swap, duplicate-top, order-reverse,
irrelevant-inject) stays positive throughout (duplicate-top/C3 $+185$
balanced / $+215$ empirical; all Holm-robust): excellent evidence stays
excellent. Contra-injection splits
the consumers (C1 $+29$n.s.\ vs C3 $+165^*$) --- provenance-awareness pays,
but only where evidence starts near-perfect.

\subsection{The tuned no-text comparator}
\label{sec:tuned}

Because a fixed prior is a weak comparator, we tuned a constant no-text
belief on development queries only (66-triple grid at step $0.1$, 40 queries,
disjoint seeds;
selection, not inference) and evaluated it
once on test: always anticipate surge, with runners-up $(0.1,0,0.9)$ and
$(0,0.1,0.9)$ at dev $J=1869.5$ behind the winner's $1885.6$ (full grid in
the frozen artifact). The top-3 dev order is preserved on test (runners-up
$J=1869.1$ vs winner $1881.1$; paired winner-minus-runner-up $+19.8$
$[+4,+20]$, exploratory outside Holm), against winner's-curse luck.
It reaches $J=1881.1$, $+102.1$ over the prior ($p<0.0002$ crossed bootstrap,
$B=5{,}000$, same paired scheme; Holm over the 9 tuned-family comparisons ---
tuned-vs-prior plus tuned-vs-each-real-C1/C3-arm --- rejects $9/9$; labeled
extension, SIVR $0.474$,
i.e.\ 47\% of the perfect-semantics value recovered) ---
and still every real-retriever arm sits below it (paired tuned-minus-arm
CIs, all excluding zero: rerank/C1 $[+108,+184]$, rerank/C3 $[+82,+165]$,
oracle-C3 $[-118,-35]$ the other way). The comparator cuts both ways:
it rules out a straw-man prior, but its edge is prior-dependent (lost at
$-39$ normal-heavy) and mechanistic (equals perfect semantics in surge worlds
since a missed unit costs $18$ against $\sim$1--3 to carry one).

\section{Boundaries and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The feed is synthetic, single-SKU and finite-regime, with constructed, not
human-assessed, relevance --- unlike the situational programmes that motivate
this work, which require real users (validation unexecuted; agent-audit
priors $0.82$/$0.90$, $\kappa=0.874$, are priors, not substitutes); absolute
nDCG values should
not be compared outside this bank. One simulator; two fixed controller
classes (primary LP plus a base-stock extension that reverses some arms),
two open-weight models from one family; the C0 pattern needs no LLM and
carries the model-free half of the claim. 160 test queries over 63 unique
texts
(effective $N\approx137$); five seeds adequate for balanced headlines
(query:seed variance $6$--$7{:}1$ in pilots; leave-one-seed-out headline
$\Delta$ within $\pm10$), not
for regime-conditional inference (exploratory). Held-out templates
unavailable; held-out entities score higher for
an untested mix reason. The tuned comparator is prior-dependent (loses under
normal-heavy weights, where BM25/C1 is $-30$ $[-93,+35]$n.s.\ and oracle-C3
$+79$ $[+6,+151]$); BM25/dense/hybrid C1 harm and oracle-LLM value point
the same way under all five weightings checked (full CIs under balanced and
empirical only). SIVR is
reported bookkeeping with signed guards, not a new metric. Nothing here
establishes deployment safety.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a decision consumer, evidence quality must be scored at the level of
induced actions and realised return: better-ranked evidence yields marginally
better beliefs yet, for LLM consumers, operating returns at or below reading
nothing on real retrieval (Holm-robust harm on every real-retriever naive-use
arm), while oracle evidence from
the same corpus is worth up to $+179$. The failure is a mismatch between
retrieval, consumer and controller --- a second fixed policy class preserves
rerank harm and oracle gains while reversing BM25/C1 and the tuned constant,
confirming harm lives at the pairing level --- located by the ladder:
selection is not the bottleneck (relevance-to-factual $0.00$),
interpretation closes part of the gap ($141 \to 36$), and control leaves the
most value unrealised ($+456.8$; this bundles demand-foresight value with
controller suboptimality, since the clairvoyant resolves sample-path
randomness the regime controller never sees). Score retrieval intended for a decision loop
against that loop, with a no-retrieval control, a tuned no-text comparator
and a matched budget --- and do not assume a relevance gain survives the
handoff.

\section{Design, accounting and reproduction}
\label{app:design}

\paragraph{Environment, costs and timing.}
Single-item lost-sales inventory over $T=40$ periods: revenue $\$10$/sale,
ordering $\$5$ fixed plus $\$2$/unit, holding $\$1$, stockout penalty $\$8$,
initial on-hand $30$, Poisson demand (mean $8$). Regimes: \textsc{normal};
\textsc{supplier delay} (lead time up in window); \textsc{demand surge}
(demand rate up). Within a period: receive arrivals, realise demand, fulfil,
order. Orders placed at $t$ under lead time $L$ arrive at $t+L$; in-transit
orders keep arrival periods. One shared schedule implementation.

\paragraph{Information release and belief to action.}
Warning at period $12$; events periods $20$--$29$. Before publication all
controllers plan under the benchmark prior; after, the interpreted belief
replaces it with state and pipeline carried over. The controller solves a
receding-horizon LP per period (SciPy \texttt{linprog}, HiGHS; flow-balance
and bound constraints with horizon 40 in the artifact), executing its first order by minimising
$\sum_\tau(2\hat{q}_\tau+\hat{I}_\tau-18\hat{s}_\tau)$, where the coefficients are the stated variable/holding
costs and the revenue-plus-stockout at stake, under
belief-weighted demand and lead time, identically across sources. The online
program is a continuous relaxation: the simulator's $\$5$ per-order fixed
cost is charged on execution but not anticipated in planning (uniform across
arms); only the hindsight reference solves the mixed-integer form with setup
binaries, so the control gap further bundles relaxation against foresight.
