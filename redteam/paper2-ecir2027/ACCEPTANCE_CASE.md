# Acceptance case (written after, and surviving, the rejection)

IR evaluation currently cannot score evidence for decision consumers:
relevance and generation-quality proxies are the only portable instruments,
and both are blind to control-relevant quality by construction. This paper
delivers the missing instrument in executable form — frozen
retrieval→belief→control with matched budgets, paired randomisation, a
six-rung oracle ladder separating selection from veracity from
interpretation from control, and a tuned no-text baseline — and uses it to
establish three facts that change evaluation practice: (i) relevance
ordering preserved into beliefs inverts into operating loss for LLM
consumers (−41…−104 Holm-robust) while oracle evidence gains (+178.9);
(ii) the loss is located, not merely observed (0.00 / ∼37–140 / +456.8);
(iii) a no-text operating point beats every tested real retriever in two
worlds, so decision-bound retrieval must be scored against that baseline.
No cited work provides this instrument or these measurements; the
limitations are stated where a reviewer can price them.
